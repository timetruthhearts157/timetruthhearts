Tiny Dancer

But, oh, how it feels so real

Lying here with no one near

Only you, and you can hear me

When I say softly, slowly

Hold me closer, tiny dancer

- Elton John, Tiny Dancer

I'd stood on the edge of this same forest in the Spring, completely unaware of how much my life would change in such a brief amount of time. Now, as the crisp edge of the Autumn breeze blew through the glen, I knew what I had to do. There was no other choice. This small lucite prison I carried contained all the hope I'd found in this world but it also contained the greatest sorrow and grief. I had to let her go. She needed to be with her people.

I was reminded of that Summer morning so many years ago I had stood on the Hill of Tara at sunrise. I thought my eyes were playing tricks on me as the Sun hung low on the sky as it rose. But no, they really had simply . . . appeared . . . there off in the distance as the hill slopped down and away. They where just . . . there . . . and then they were comsumed with a ritualistic dance whose music I could not hear. All shapes and sizes, bathed in an etheral glow. I was transfixed by their dance. It seemed to grip me tightly, almost pressing the air out of my chest. I felt a strong, undeniable compulsion to cross the distance and join them.

Just as my legs started to move, almost out of their own volition, a strong weathered hand came down on my shoulder. The spell had been broken then and when I looked up I saw the face of experience and knowledge staring back at me.

"Aye boy," he said in a thick Celtic brogue, "the Daoine Sí. The Good People. They call to you."
