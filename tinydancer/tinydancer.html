Tiny Dancer

But, oh, how it feels so real

Lying here with no one near

Only you, and you can hear me

When I say softly, slowly

Hold me closer, tiny dancer

- Elton John, Tiny Dancer

I'd stood on the edge of this same forest in the Spring, completely unaware of how much my life would change in such a brief amount of time. Now, as the crisp edge of the Autumn breeze blew through the glen, I knew what I had to do. There was no other choice. This small lucite prison I carried contained all the hope I'd found in this world but it also contained the greatest sorrow and grief. I had to let her go. She needed to be with her people.

I was reminded of that Summer morning so many years ago I had stood on the Hill of Tara at sunrise. I thought my eyes were playing tricks on me as the Sun hung low on the sky as it rose. But no, they really had simply . . . appeared . . . there off in the distance as the hill slopped down and away. They where just . . . there . . . and then they were comsumed with a ritualistic dance whose music I could not hear. All shapes and sizes, bathed in an etheral glow. I was transfixed by their dance. It seemed to grip me tightly, almost pressing the air out of my chest. I felt a strong, undeniable compulsion to cross the distance and join them.

Just as my legs started to move, almost out of their own volition, a strong weathered hand came down on my shoulder. The spell had been broken then and when I looked up I saw the face of experience and knowledge staring back at me.

"Aye boy," he said in a thick Celtic brogue, "the Tuatha Dé Danann. The Good People. They call to you."

The stranger produced a hand rolled cigarette almost magically and lit it up. He sniffed, took a drag, but maintained his firm grip on my shoulder.

"Midsummer," he sniffed again, "One of the Moving Days. If I let you go with them, you might not know where in the world you'd end up."

Thinking back to that day, as I stand on this wooded threshold, I never really understood what happened that morning. The older I got the more willing I became to think that it was some sort of flight of fancy or liminal, imaginary event. But that warm May Day six months ago proved to me that it had been a real thing that happened in a Universe that was seemingly stranger than anything I could contain in my mind. A world that contained possibilities more fantastical than I had hoped. She proved all of that.

Steeling myself, I picked up the lucite box and marched across the border between the field and the woods. The electricity in the air immediately made every hair on my body stand up. This place always felt different, super charged with hope and fear and light and dark. But six months ago something changed, something had shifted that morning I ventured out here with Molly. Molly could feel it too; she snarled and snapped and I had to drag her into the woods. And once she was in there, she stuck close to me; either in fear or protection. I couldn't tell at the time.
